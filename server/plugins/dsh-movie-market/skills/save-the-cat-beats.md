# Beat Sheet (Save-the-Cat Style)

A beat sheet is a pacing scaffold for a literary script: a fixed sequence of
story beats with rough positions in a ~110-page feature. Use it to find where a
script sags or rushes, not as a formula to fill mechanically.

## The fifteen beats

1. **Opening Image** (~p.1) — a visual thesis of the world before change.
2. **Theme Stated** (~p.5) — someone voices the lesson the hero must learn.
3. **Setup** (~p.1–10) — the ordinary world, the want, the flaw, the stakes.
4. **Catalyst** (~p.12) — the inciting incident disrupts the status quo.
5. **Debate** (~p.12–25) — hesitation; can/should the hero act?
6. **Break into Two** (~p.25) — a choice launches the hero into the new world.
7. **B Story** (~p.30) — a relationship (often the love story) carries the theme.
8. **Fun and Games** (~p.30–55) — the "promise of the premise"; the set pieces.
9. **Midpoint** (~p.55) — a false victory or false defeat raises the stakes.
10. **Bad Guys Close In** (~p.55–75) — external pressure and internal doubt mount.
11. **All Is Lost** (~p.75) — the lowest point; a whiff of death.
12. **Dark Night of the Soul** (~p.75–85) — grief before the final insight.
13. **Break into Three** (~p.85) — the theme crystallizes into a plan.
14. **Finale** (~p.85–110) — the hero executes, proves change, and wins/loses on
    their own choice.
15. **Final Image** (~p.110) — a visual antithesis of the opening; proof of change.

## Working steps

1. Place every existing scene against the nearest beat.
2. Note beats with no scene (gaps) and scenes that serve no beat (fat).
3. Check the Catalyst lands early enough and the Midpoint genuinely reverses.
4. Verify All Is Lost is the emotional floor, not just a plot setback.
5. Confirm Opening and Final Images mirror each other to bookend the change.

## Output

A beat-to-scene map with page targets, flagging gaps to write and fat to trim.
