# Shot Types and Framing

A shot plan is a sequence of deliberate framings, not a coverage checklist. This
skill maps shot size, angle, and composition to intent so every shot in the plan
says something about the story beat it serves.

## Shot sizes and their default meaning

- **Extreme Wide (EWS)** — place, scale, isolation; establish geography.
- **Wide (WS)** — the full body in its environment; blocking and relationships.
- **Medium (MS)** — waist up; dialogue and everyday connection.
- **Medium Close-Up (MCU)** — chest up; the workhorse of conversation.
- **Close-Up (CU)** — face only; emotion, decision, reaction.
- **Extreme Close-Up (ECU)** — a detail (eye, hand, object); intensity or clue.

## Angles

- **Eye level** — neutrality, parity.
- **Low angle** — power, dominance, heroism.
- **High angle** — vulnerability, diminishment.
- **Dutch/tilted** — unease, disorientation.
- **Over-the-shoulder (OTS)** — the standard dialogue axis.
- **POV** — subjectivity; put the audience in a character's eyes.

## Framing tools

- **Rule of thirds vs. centre** — centre for formality/power, thirds for ease.
- **Headroom and lead room** — respect eyeline direction; short-side framing
  creates tension.
- **Depth staging** — foreground/midground/background layers add dimension.
- **Negative space** — emptiness around a subject reads as loneliness or threat.

## Working steps

1. For each beat, state the emotion or information the shot must deliver.
2. Pick the smallest shot size that carries it (avoid over-tightening too early).
3. Choose an angle that reinforces the power dynamic of the beat.
4. Note composition (framing, depth, movement hook) for the storyboard pass.

## Output

A shot list annotating each shot with size, angle, composition intent, and the
beat it serves.
