# Camera Movement and Lens Choice

Movement and optics are storytelling tools. This skill helps a shot plan specify
*motivated* camera moves (movement with a reason) and lens choices that shape how
a shot feels, so the plan reads as direction rather than a list of tricks.

## Camera moves and their motivation

- **Pan / Tilt** — reveal or follow within a fixed position; connect two subjects.
- **Dolly / Truck** — move with or toward a subject; a **dolly-in** focuses
  attention or marks a realization.
- **Crane / Jib** — scale, transitions, lifting out of a scene.
- **Handheld** — immediacy, anxiety, documentary presence.
- **Steadicam / Gimbal** — fluid following without handheld shake.
- **Zoom** — optical magnification; a slow zoom can feel voyeuristic, a **crash
  zoom** is emphatic or comic.
- **Dolly zoom (vertigo)** — dolly against zoom to warp perspective; use sparingly.
- **Arc** — circle a subject to build energy or show all sides.

Rule: every move should start and end on a motivated frame; unmotivated movement
reads as fidgeting.

## Lens and focal length

- **Wide (e.g. 18–35mm)** — deep space, environment, exaggeration of distance;
  can distort faces up close.
- **Normal (e.g. 50mm)** — closest to human perspective; natural, unobtrusive.
- **Telephoto (e.g. 85–200mm)** — compresses depth, isolates a subject, flattens
  backgrounds; intimacy or surveillance.
- **Aperture / depth of field** — shallow (wide open) isolates; deep (stopped
  down) keeps context sharp for staged depth.

## Working steps

1. For each shot, ask what the audience's attention should do — that motivates
   the move (or the decision to hold still).
2. Choose a focal length for the intended compression and intimacy.
3. Note the start and end framing of any move.
4. Flag moves that need rigging (crane, dolly, gimbal) for scheduling.

## Output

A movement-and-lens annotation per shot: move (with motivation), focal length,
aperture/depth intent, and any rigging requirement.
