# Scene Craft and Subtext Dialogue

This skill polishes a literary script scene by scene. Two rules carry most of
the craft: **enter late, leave early**, and **let dialogue mean something other
than what it says**. Subtext is the gap between a line and the want driving it.

## Scene-level craft

- **Enter late.** Start at the last possible moment before the turn; cut
  greetings, arrivals, and throat-clearing.
- **Leave early.** End on the beat that changes the value, then get out.
- **One scene, one turn.** If two values change, consider two scenes.
- **Anchor the reader** with a single concrete detail of place, then move.

## Writing subtext

1. For each character in the scene, state the want they are pursuing *right now*.
2. Ask what they are avoiding saying; that avoidance is the subtext.
3. Convert direct statements into oblique ones — deflection, questions, jokes,
   silence, or talk about a surrogate subject.
4. Let status shift across the exchange: someone gains or loses ground.
5. Read it aloud; if a line states the theme outright, cut or bury it.

## Dialogue checks

- Distinct voices: cover the names — can you still tell who is speaking?
- No "on-the-nose" exposition delivered to a character who already knows it.
- Conflict present: agreement is only interesting when it costs something.
- Rhythm: vary line length; use beats and interruptions.

## Output

A revised scene with annotated subtext (want/avoidance per line) and a note on
where it enters and exits relative to its turn.
