# Three-Act Structure

Use this skill when a literary script feels flat or meandering and needs a
load-bearing spine. It converts a premise into three acts joined by two
turning points, so every scene pulls toward a decision the protagonist cannot
undo.

## When to apply

- A script has vivid scenes but no forward pressure.
- The ending arrives without the audience feeling the cost.
- You need a shared vocabulary to discuss pacing with collaborators.

## The spine

### Act I — Setup (roughly the first quarter)

Establish the ordinary world, the protagonist's want, and the flaw that will
make the journey hard. End Act I on the **inciting incident** and a **point of
no return**: a choice that locks the protagonist into the story question.

### Act II — Confrontation (the middle half)

Raise stakes through progressive complications. The protagonist tries, fails,
adapts. Place a **midpoint reversal** that changes what the character believes
they are fighting for. Let the act end at the **lowest point**, where the old
strategy is exhausted.

### Act III — Resolution (the last quarter)

Force a final choice that tests the flaw directly. The climax answers the story
question; the denouement shows the new equilibrium and its price.

## Working steps

1. Write the story question in one sentence ("Will X achieve Y despite Z?").
2. Mark the two turning points before drafting any scene.
3. For each scene, state which act pressure it serves; cut scenes that serve none.
4. Check that stakes rise monotonically across Act II.
5. Confirm the climax is caused by a character decision, not coincidence.

## Output

A beat outline labelling every scene with its act, its turning-point role, and
the stake it changes — ready to hand to a shot-plan skill for coverage.
